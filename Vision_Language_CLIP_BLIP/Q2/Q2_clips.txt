BLIP aligns image regions with text using a visual encoder (ViT) that produces patch-level embeddings, which are then cross-attended to by a text decoder. The image-grounded text encoder uses cross-attention at each layer to fuse visual and textual features, allowing token representations to be conditioned on specific spatial regions of the image. However, BLIP lacks explicit bounding-box supervision, so its spatial reasoning is implicit — it can often identify what is present but struggles with precise where (e.g., left/right/behind relationships).

BLIP gives broadly consistent answers for direct location questions but becomes vaguer or shifts phrasing for relational questions (e.g., "relative to the man"), suggesting it relies more on language priors than precise spatial grounding.
